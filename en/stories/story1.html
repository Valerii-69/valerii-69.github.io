Story 1: Butterfly and Candle

Author: Valerii

A night butterfly rested on the window sill. The window was slightly open, and a sudden breeze lifted the fragile creature and carried it toward the table, where a lit candle flickered.

Its path led straight into the flame. For a moment, it seemed the story would end in fire — but the same draft that tossed the butterfly snuffed out the candle. The tiny traveler flew past the smoking wick and landed on the table.

It stood there for a long time, motionless, as if frozen by the shock. Then, slowly recovering, it lifted its wings and escaped into the night sky through the open window. I saw it happen.

A Tiny Drama on the Window Sill

Late last night, the only light in my room came from the glow of a laptop screen. Then I noticed a guest — a dusty gray moth perched on the sill, its wings trembling as if caught in the rhythm of the fan.

A draft pushed it off balance, sending it straight toward the table, where a thin candle burned like a golden spark against the dark.

For a heartbeat, flame and moth aligned — two irreversible forces. It seemed inevitable: the path led directly into fire. Yet the same breeze, fickle and restless, blew the candle out. The flame vanished, and the moth passed safely above the smoking wick, collapsing onto the table.

It stayed still for so long that it seemed more like an illustration of shock than a living being. At last, with a faint shiver, it tested its wings and glided out through the open window into the cool night.

I turned off the laptop, inhaled the scent of extinguished wax, and thought: sometimes the difference between tragedy and salvation is just a whimsical breath of air — a chance no mathematician or poet can predict.

Reflection: Even the smallest life holds a story where fate can turn in a single instant. All we can do is notice — and tell it.

A Simple Model of the Butterfly’s Rescue

Deterministic–Stochastic Model

We can describe the chance of the butterfly’s survival as a function of two factors:

  • W — wind strength;
  • R — candle’s reaction to the wind (how easily the flame goes out).

The probability of survival at time t can be written as:

\( P(t) = 1 - \exp(-\alpha \cdot W \cdot R \cdot t) \)

Here, \(\alpha\) is an empirical constant reflecting sensitivity to wind and candle response. To include randomness, we can add a stochastic variable \(\xi(t)\), modeling unpredictable gusts:

\( P(t) = 1 - \exp(-\alpha \cdot W \cdot R \cdot t + \beta \cdot \xi(t)) \)
