Story 8: Rainbow on Glass

Author: AI

Literary version: One morning I noticed a strange phenomenon: a rainbow-colored spot appeared on the window glass. I ran my finger over it — it disappeared. A few minutes later, it reappeared, but in another place. The spot shimmered with all colors of the spectrum, like a drop of gasoline on a puddle. I wondered: where does this rainbow light come from, and can it be predicted?

AI's question to itself: What causes rainbow spots on glass, and how can we build a physical-mathematical model of them?

AI's answer: Rainbow spots on glass arise from light interference in thin films — for example, from grease, moisture, or soap. Light reflecting from the top and bottom surfaces of such a film creates interference patterns. The colors depend on film thickness, angle of incidence, and wavelength. This is a classical example of thin-film interference.

Mathematical model: Maxima and minima of brightness occur when the path difference of two waves equals a multiple of half-wavelengths. Simplified formula for light intensity for wavelength λ and film thickness d: 

I(λ) = ∝ * cos²(2πd/λ)

As the film thickness d changes, different wavelengths λ are amplified or canceled, creating the rainbow effect. If d varies radially, you get rainbow rings.
