Dancing Raisins

Observe how raisins rise and fall in carbonated water.

Experiment description:
Take a transparent glass or jar with carbonated water (mineral or sweet, doesn't matter). Drop a few raisins into it. Within seconds, you'll see the raisins "dance": they rise to the surface and then sink again.
What happens?
Gas bubbles (carbon dioxide) stick to the surface of the raisins, making them float.
When they reach the surface, bubbles burst — the raisin becomes heavy again and sinks.
The process repeats several times until the gas leaves the water.
What you can investigate:
Count how many times a raisin rises and falls before stopping.
Compare raisins with other items: rice grains, small pasta pieces, tiny beads.
Try different carbonated drinks and compare the "dance energy".
Mathematical model (sketch):
Rise is related to the net force:
F_buoyancy = F_Archimedes − F_gravity
where the raisin’s weight decreases when bubbles stick to it.
The number of bubbles can be estimated as:
N × V_bubble
(number of bubbles multiplied by their volume).
