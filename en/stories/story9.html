Story 9: Bald Eagle

Author: Valeriy

I was in 8th grade when my father brought home a young bald eagle from the taiga. He was a military man.
The soldiers had found the eagle; it was nearly frozen. The winter was very cold.
My parents placed it on a wardrobe. At first, it didn’t eat anything.
It sat in the far corner; the wardrobe stood in the corner of the room, and it wouldn’t let anyone near.
We lived in a communal apartment and ate at a table by the window.
At that time, the room seemed enormous, but now I realize it was about 9 meters long.
One day, while eating with the whole family, the eagle flew to the table and demanded that I give it a piece of meat.
At that time, not everyone had refrigerators, and many families stored food in winter on homemade shelves outside the window.
I reached out the window, took a small piece of lamb wrapped in paper, sat down at the table, and began unwrapping it. The eagle approached and began helping with its beak.
When we finally managed, I cut a small piece and offered it — it didn’t eat immediately, just watched the meat and me.
After a while, I realized the meat was cold. I held it in my hand for about 10 minutes to warm it. When I next offered it to the eagle, it took it in its beak,
moved to a free spot on the table, pressed the meat with its claw, and began eating small pieces.
This was the first time I fed the eagle.
Then the eagle began coming to the table every time we ate together. On days when we ate separately, it stayed on the wardrobe, and I left food for it there before school.
I was amazed that the eagle always sat at exactly the same spot.
I wondered how the bird “calculated” its flight trajectory in a 9-meter room.
In spring, we placed the eagle on a shelf outside the window. It stayed there for two days, pecking at the glass. I fed it through the window. We did not see it fly away.

Literary version from the eagle’s perspective

I remember darkness, icy air, pain in my wing… Then — warmth. They brought me into a bright room. It smelled of wood, bread, and people. I sat on a high nest — they called it a "wardrobe." I didn’t eat — I waited. Waited for a signal that I could trust. Then came the day when everyone gathered at the table. I sensed the aroma of food — meat. I planned my descent. The boy (my friend) offered me food, but it was cold as the taiga air. I looked into his eyes. He understood. He warmed the meat in his hand and gave it to me. I pecked. Since then, I came every time the flock gathered. I always sat in the same spot — it was mine. I never flew unnecessarily. In spring, I saw the sun and sky. They placed me outside the window. I tapped with my beak to say goodbye. Then spread my wings and… flew away.
