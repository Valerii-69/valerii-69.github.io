Maiden Dreams - Three Synthesizers

Literary Essay

A GIRL'S DREAMS. She slept, and her dreams resounded. Not words, not images, but colors that sang softly, like the breath of water. First, there was a blue chord, like a light touch on glass. From it, circles were born, spreading across the surface of the night. Then a yellow splash, short and sharp, like the glance of a violin. And then a greenish bass, deep, as if the earth were remembering its first sunrise. The girl heard all this—and knew: every color, every sound, was connected by a number. She saw seven lines of light converge at seven points, forming 49 shining squares, each resounding with its own dream. When she touched them, space began to breathe. Waves emanated from the center—not through air, but through meaning, as if the mind itself had become an instrument. The dream formed into music, and the music into form. Thus, labyrinths of light appeared, where one could hear geometry, sense notes, and see numbers. When she woke up, the girl couldn't remember the melody—but colors still shimmered behind her eyelids. She knew: if she pressed a single button, the dream would return—in the form of sound. Thus was born a project in which music and mathematics merge like day and night at dawn. In the silence of the night, three synthesizers create waves of music, as if the girls' dreams were mingling in harmony. Each note is a flash of memory, each pause a breath of space. Is it possible to visualize these dreams, hear their form, and construct a model of sensations?

Mathematical Model

Each synthesizer constructs a sequence of notes based on harmonic series. Let the notes A, B, C correspond to three series: {C4, E4, G4}, {D4, F4, A4}, {E4, G4, B4}. Frequencies are calculated via MIDI: freq = 440 * 2^((midi-69)/12).
